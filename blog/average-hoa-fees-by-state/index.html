Average HOA Fees by State in 2026 — Full 50-State Breakdown

Published May 12, 2026 • 10 min read

Imagine purchasing a gorgeous condominium nestled near Honolulu's shoreline. You've budgeted carefully for the mortgage, only to find yourself hit with an average HOA payment of $790 every single month. Meanwhile, across the Gulf Coast in Mississippi, a homeowner inside a master-planned neighborhood pays a modest $140 monthly fee to cover the exact same housing category.

This massive chasm represents one of the most overlooked realities of the American real estate landscape. Mandatory association dues have quieted down as a background line item, but in 2026, they stand as a principal driving force behind overall cost of living shifts.

Why HOA Fees Vary So Wildly by State

Homeowners associations across the country aren't corporate copies of one another. Every state features a highly distinct cocktail of natural risks, local regulations, labor conditions, and historical building philosophies. Levers including climate damage, structural density (high-rises with expensive master policies vs suburban detached plots with simple perimeter signs), and legislative mandates (like California's Davis-Stirling and Florida's Senate Bill 4-D) dictate local costs.

The Most Expensive States for HOA Fees (2026 Estimates)

  • Hawaii (Average $790/mo): High isolated logistics premiums, salt-mist corrosion, and ultra-dense resort facilities.
  • New York (Average $480/mo): High co-op concentrations, elevator regulatory filings, and union concierge wages.
  • California (Average $450/mo): Davis-Stirling regulations, high labor rates, and skyrocketing wildfire master insurance premiums.
  • Florida (Average $410/mo): Senate Bill 4-D Structural Integrity reserve studies and severe ocean wind premium hikes.

The Cheapest States for HOA Fees

  • Mississippi (Average $140/mo): Simple single-family plots dominate, nominal amenities.
  • Arkansas (Average $145/mo): Minimal shared elements, low construction overhead.
  • West Virginia (Average $155/mo): High concentration of resident self-managed developments.
