HOA Fees in California: Why They're the Highest in the Nation

Published May 15, 2026 • 7 min read

California sits near the top of national real estate cost lists. Property buyers across San Francisco, Los Angeles, and San Diego discover that monthly association dues act as a major secondary mortgage. Under local law, specific structures dictate everything from wildfire and earthquake insurance requirements to solar panels.

1. Regional HOA Averages in California

  • Bay Area (San Francisco/San Jose): Average sits between $450 - $750/mo. The older building frames and high union manual contractor wages drive up operational requirements.
  • Southern California (Los Angeles/San Diego): Average oscillates between $350 - $600/mo. Luxury coastal treatments and pool systems add significant burdens.
  • Sacramento / Central Valley: Average sits closer to $250 - $350/mo where single-family detached subdivisions dominate.

2. The Davis-Stirling Act and Homeowner Protections

California's common interest developments must strictly comply with the Davis-Stirling Common Interest Development Act. This code commands detailed reserve calculations and annual disclosure checks. However, it also protects homeowners, prohibiting boards from outlawing solar roof panels or low-water Xeriscaping.

3. The Master Insurance Premium Squeeze

Due to severe climate and wildfire exposures across Southern and Northern California, major commercial carriers have dropped state coverage layers. Consequently, associations' master property policy premiums have multiplied, forcing instant regular fee hikes to bridge the gap.
