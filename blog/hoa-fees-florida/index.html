HOA Fees in Florida: What Every Buyer Must Know Before Closing

Published May 13, 2026 • 7 min read

Florida holds a striking real estate distinction that surprises many newcomers: the Sunshine State is home to the highest concentration of homeowners association (HOA) and condominium communities per capita in the entire United States. With over 48,000 active community associations representing upwards of 9.6 million residents, managed communities are not just an option in Florida—they are the default vehicle of modern residential homeownership.

1. Florida HOA Fee Averages by City

The financial footprint of a Florida homeowner association varies enormously based on geography:

  • Miami Metro Area ($400 - $600/mo): Highest cost concentration due to beach elevator maintenance and structural ocean wind systems.
  • Orlando Metro Area ($200 - $350/mo): Resort-style amenities, water slide loops, and guards require steady contractor layers.
  • Tampa Bay Area ($250 - $400/mo): Coastal mid-rises combine with suburban inland developments.
  • Jacksonville Area ($150 - $300/mo): Sprawling single-family configurations make operating costs cheaper.

2. Landmark Laws: Florida Senate Bill 4-D

The passage of Senate Bill 4-D changed Florida condo associations completely. Prior to the law, boards could waive general reserve contributions. Under SB 4-D, milestone inspections are mandatory at 30 years for buildings over 3 stories. Furthermore, boards are forbidden from underfunding structural integrity reserves, prompting immediate fee inflation to fill previous deficits.

3. Post-Surfside Special Assessments

In the wake of Surfside, cities have become extremely risk-averse. Severe spalling balcony concrete or structural rebar rust must be immediately patched by owners. If reserves run dry, associations issue lump-sum special assessments costing $15,000–$40,000 per homeowner, presenting massive financial flags to buyers.
