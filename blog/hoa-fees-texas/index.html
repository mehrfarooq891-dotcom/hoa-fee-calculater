HOA Fees in Texas: Complete City-by-City Guide 2026

Published May 17, 2026 • 8 min read

Texas has become a primary target for relocation. In the Lone Star State, vast master-planned single-family neighborhoods dominate suburban landscapes. However, developers utilize distinct management covenants to regulate everything from exterior brick ratios to recreational swim-complex pools.

1. Texas HOA Averages by City

  • Austin (Average $320/mo): Premium tech-suburb layouts and community centers carry higher operational overhead.
  • Dallas (Average $295/mo): Sprawling swim/tennis subdivisions are the regional housing standard.
  • Houston (Average $280/mo): Humid coastal weather structural foundation repairs keep operational budgets active.
  • San Antonio (Average $230/mo): Lean administrative charges keep municipal averages highly favorable.

2. Texas Property Code Chapter 209

The core of homeowner protections in Texas resides within Texas Property Code Chapter 209 (Texas Residential Property Owners Protection Act). Under this statutory framework, homeowners enjoy extensive shields restricting sudden board foreclosure actions. Additionally, members can access detailed ledgers and host open elections.

3. The Developer-to-Member Transition Gap

In newly built Texas master subdivisions, developers retain control of the board until a specific share of unit lots sell. During this builder phase, fees are kept artificially low. After transition to home owners occurs, the true administrative and plowing costs hit, causing major regular dues spikes.
