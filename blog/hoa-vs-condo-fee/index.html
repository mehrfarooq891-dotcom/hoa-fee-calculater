HOA vs Condo Fee: What's the Difference and Which Costs More?

Published May 20, 2026 • 8 min read

Many homebuyers use the terms "HOA fee" and "condo fee" interchangeably. However, they represent completely distinct legal concepts, financial coverage templates, and structural asset boundaries. Knowing these key definitions before you make an offer on a condominium or family home can save you thousands of dollars in unexpected capital replacement outlays.

1. The Crucial Legal Distinctions

  • HOA (Homeowners Association Fee): Usually covers properties where members individually own the physical lot space and framing (e.g. single-family detached subdivisions or townhomes). The association manages and bills for perimeter spaces and community streets.
  • COA (Condominium Association Fee): Governs units inside multi-family properties where individual buyers own only the interior drywall spacing ("walls-in"). The association owns and manages the entire physical building envelope, exterior walls, roofs, elevators, and corridors.

2. Which Fee Costs More and Why?

Condo association fees are consistently higher than standard single-family homeowners dues. While a typical suburban HOA might range from $40 to $150 monthly, a condominium board in the same area often registers $300 to $700+ per month. The cause is simple: condo boards carry total structural responsibility for major building components, whereas suburban single-family property owners patch their own siding and roofs direct.
